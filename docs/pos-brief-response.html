3 · The camera pad: the real project

This is the part of your brief that is genuinely new, and the part your Nu 500 must ultimately pay back. Your instinct is right on the two points that matter most: the work runs on the till PC (so glances cost nothing monthly), and the camera never invents prices — it only proposes, and the cashier confirms. We hold you to both.

We also start ahead: our POS already does on-till product recognition — matching camera crops against the shop’s own product photos, stored locally. The green pad makes that easier, not harder: a plain background, fixed height, even light.

What the pad will honestly do

Cut goods off the green, read a barcode when it faces up, match each item to this shop’s photos, guess the unit by size (Case / Pcs / Pack), count what’s on the mat, and put a proposal on screen — your 1 Case + 3 bottles + 3 pkt scene is exactly the v0 demo.

Where it will ask for a tap

Look-alike pouches, glare, torn or brand-new packaging. Then it shows the top 3 and the cashier taps one — a second, not a search. Each tap teaches the shop’s catalog, so the pad gets sharper with use.

“Photograph packs once” is a real job

Each shop’s owner photographs every pack — a few angles each — before the pad can name it, and re-shoots when a brand changes its packaging. Plan an afternoon per shop at the start. We will make the capture flow fast, but the discipline is the shop’s.

Accuracy is won on the counter

Matte green kept clean, both LED bars on, camera locked at its height. A shop that respects the setup gets a pad that feels like a shopkeeper’s eye; a shop that doesn’t gets top-3 taps all day. The hardware list in your brief is correct — Nu 7,000–11,000 once per pad.
